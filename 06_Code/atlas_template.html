Scope of the evidence
Mapped availability does not measure visits or social connection. PLACES estimates and the adjusted models share demographic and socioeconomic information. Regression intervals condition on published health point estimates.
Sources, definitions and scenario assumptions

Historical source vintages

Mapped cafes: qualifying amenity=cafe objects in the OSM snapshot at 2022-12-31 23:59:59 UTC, after the recorded conservative cross-type duplicate rule. Counts were assigned using full tract geometry. Polygon cafe locations are bounding-box midpoints. Availability is not attendance, coffee use, quality or opening hours.

Modeled health: CDC PLACES 2024 release, with the study health measures based on 2022 BRFSS. Frequent mental distress means 14 or more mentally unhealthy days during the previous 30 days. Published tract intervals shown here are CDC model intervals.

Context and denominator: ACS 2018–2022 through CDC/ATSDR SVI 2022. Cafe density uses the total ACS resident population; health prevalence refers to adults. Poverty and unemployment percentages retain their source-specific denominators.

Boundaries: US Census Bureau TIGER/Line 2023 for analysis; Census cartographic tract geometry, simplified by 80 m in EPSG:5070 and then converted to WGS84, for display. Areas excluded from the complete-case sample are omitted. The study covers 50 populous counties in the contiguous US, not the whole country.

Nearby cafes: straight-line distance from the full-resolution tract polygon centroid to retained cafe points in the frozen expanded county extracts. Counts within 500 m and 1,000 m can include cafes in adjacent or analytically excluded tracts. These do not estimate walking routes or individual access. Centroids can be far from residential concentrations.

Reading the map

Source-layer limits are pooled across all study tracts and stay fixed when counties change. Cafe zeros use a separate light color; positive cafe-density colors cap at the pooled 99th percentile. Health and context colors use pooled 1st–99th percentile limits. Values outside caps keep their original values in the profile and CSV. Distribution bars summarize displayed tracts without population weights.

The joint-pattern view divides cafes into zero, lower positive density and higher positive density (cut at the pooled median among mapped-cafe tracts), and mental distress into pooled thirds. It describes co-location; it is not adjusted evidence of a health association.

Association scenario

The model term contrast is β × [log₂(1 + x′) − log₂(1 + x)], where x and x′ are mapped cafes per 10,000 ACS residents. Neighborhood and county terms are held fixed. The neighborhood-context model is the manuscript's extended model; the second additionally adjusts for non-cafe hospitality density.

The displayed 95% interval multiplies the county-clustered coefficient limits by that exposure difference, sorting the endpoints when the difference is negative. It reflects coefficient uncertainty only. It excludes PLACES source uncertainty, cafe-map error and individual or intervention uncertainty. The model's small added explanatory contribution is reported with the coefficients.

Targets outside the pooled observed cafe-density range are hatched and excluded from scenario color summaries. Targets above the observed 99th percentile are flagged as sparse-support values. Being within this one-dimensional range does not establish joint covariate support or transportability. No scenario value is added to a tract's PLACES prevalence.
