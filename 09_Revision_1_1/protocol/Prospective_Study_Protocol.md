# Cafe access use and social interaction in adult mental health

## Proposed prospective study

This protocol separates residential access to cafes from visits, social interaction and independently reported health. It is a proposed follow-up to the ecological study. Recruitment has not started, no participant data have been collected, and no ethics approval or funding has been obtained for this protocol. The design should be finalized with local investigators and community representatives before registration and ethics review.

## Research questions and target population

The primary question is whether changes in an adult's cafe use are associated with subsequent changes in mentally unhealthy days over 12 months, after accounting for earlier health and measured personal and neighborhood circumstances. A secondary question asks whether visits involving welcome social contact differ from solitary visits. Residential cafe availability is a separate exposure. These questions concern associations unless the additional assumptions required for causal identification can be defended.

Recruit adults aged 18 years or older through a probability-based residential address sample in selected urban neighborhoods. Sample across four combinations of neighborhood deprivation and externally verified cafe availability. Include nonusers and people with mobility, cost or language barriers. Recruiting only inside cafes would exclude nonusers and make attendance-related selection especially severe. Venue intercept interviews can inform a separate qualitative component but must not replace the residential sampling frame.

## Exposure verification

Create a dated establishment inventory using licenses, field visits and mapped businesses. Use a written definition of cafes and retain takeaway counters, tea shops and mixed restaurant-cafe businesses as separate categories. Record opening/closure dates, hours, seating, minimum purchase expectations, typical prices, step-free access, toilets, noise and opportunities to remain without repeated purchases. Record attempted visits and inaccessible venues. A second trained observer should independently assess a random subset; report agreement and resolve disagreements without viewing health responses.

Estimate walk-network distance and reachable establishments within 400 m and 800 m of residences. Those distances are design choices, not established causal boundaries. Compare these measures with participants' reported activity spaces and actual destinations. Update the inventory at baseline, 6 months and 12 months; record intermediate closures and openings. Use neighborhood population and daytime demand measures only when their definitions and years are documented.

## Measurements and schedule

Collect baseline health and covariates before prospective follow-up. Repeat surveys at 3, 6 and 12 months. At each wave, use a 7-day diary recording visits, duration, spending, whether the participant was alone or accompanied, planned versus incidental contact, perceived welcome, and positive or negative interaction. Record coffee/caffeine and alcohol consumption separately from venue use. Nonusers complete the same diary and can report time in parks, libraries, community centers and other gathering places.

The primary health variable is the number of mentally unhealthy days in the preceding 30 days using the CDC question underlying frequent mental distress. Analyze the day count as the primary outcome; the threshold of 14 or more days is a secondary outcome for comparability with PLACES. Use the original validated wording and an appropriate validated language version. A separate validated loneliness measure can assess social experience, with instrument permissions and scoring verified before use. Do not rename loneliness, diagnosed depression or K6 distress as frequent mental distress.

Baseline and repeated covariates should include age, gender, education, employment, household resources, living arrangements, mobility limitations, pre-existing mental-health conditions, treatment, life events, residential moves and reasons for choosing the neighborhood or a venue. Record visit preferences and baseline social networks. Income, mobility and prior distress may affect both attendance and health. Confounder selection must follow an explicit causal diagram before examining results; mediators such as new social interaction should not be placed in the total-association model by default.

## Estimation and interpretation

Define the primary contrast before registration, for example a within-person difference of one cafe visit per week between waves in relation to the next wave's mentally unhealthy-day count. Fit a longitudinal model that accounts for repeated observations within people and neighborhood clustering, calendar wave, earlier outcome values and prespecified time-varying covariates. Check the bounded and zero-heavy day-count distribution before choosing the final likelihood. Present absolute predicted differences with intervals and observed support for the contrast.

Use survey design and nonresponse weights when supported by the residential sampling design. Retain all recorded exposure categories and distinguish a missing diary from zero visits. Prespecify multiple imputation for plausibly missing-at-random covariates and compare with complete-case results; use attrition weighting and sensitivity scenarios for informative loss to follow-up. These methods do not eliminate unmeasured selection or reverse causation.

Temporal mediation would require cafe use to precede measured interaction and interaction to precede health assessment, together with assumptions about exposure–outcome, mediator–outcome and exposure–mediator confounding. Register this as a separate secondary analysis only if the measurement schedule and sample support it. A cross-sectional association between visits and loneliness is not a mediation test. Time-varying confounders affected by earlier visits may require methods beyond conventional covariate adjustment.

## Openings and closures as a possible extension

A documented opening or closure could support a controlled natural-experiment component. Establish several pre-event outcome measurements and comparable unexposed neighborhoods, examine pre-event trajectories, and account for concurrent redevelopment, relocation and changes in other amenities. Prespecify exposure distance, anticipation and spillover handling. Openings are not randomly assigned; neither difference-in-differences nor a pretrend test automatically establishes causality. If enough eligible events and baseline measurements cannot be obtained, do not present this component as an intervention evaluation.

## Feasibility and sample size

Start with a feasibility pilot of approximately 60 adults across at least 6 neighborhoods, including nonusers. This is a proposed operational target, not an adequately powered health-effect study. Evaluate recruitment yield, diary completion, follow-up retention, exposure variation, instrument acceptability and intraclass correlations. Examine reasons for nonparticipation across socioeconomic groups.

Before the main study, simulate power and precision for the registered longitudinal estimand using plausible individual-level outcome variance, within-person correlation, neighborhood clustering, nonuser share, differential attrition and the smallest difference judged substantively meaningful with stakeholders. Compare at least 30, 60 and 90 neighborhoods with different numbers of participants per neighborhood and 15–30% attrition scenarios. Report interval widths as well as power. Do not power the cohort from the ecological coefficient of −0.096 percentage points; it is a different exposure scale, unit and outcome-generating process. The main sample size remains to be justified after pilot parameters are available.

## Ethics privacy and governance

Obtain local research-ethics approval, informed consent and a participant distress-response procedure before recruitment. Participation and compensation must not require cafe purchases. Precise home addresses should be stored separately under restricted access; derive exposure measures and minimize retention of identifiable location data. Optional location tracking requires separate consent and must have a diary-based alternative. Do not publish raw movement traces or venue-linked individual mental-health records.

Preregister the final protocol, primary estimand, exclusions, analysis, missing-data strategy and multiplicity policy before main-study outcome analysis. Archive code, blank instruments and an appropriately aggregated or synthetic demonstration dataset after disclosure review; label synthetic examples clearly. A prospective study using independently collected health can address the principal source-model limitation of PLACES, while still requiring careful treatment of selection and confounding.
